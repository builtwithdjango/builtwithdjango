# Product

## Users

Built with Django serves:

- Django-curious builders and students looking for credible examples.
- Working developers and founders researching what Django can support.
- Makers who want their products discovered and represented accurately.
- Hiring teams and candidates looking for Django work and talent.
- Community members looking for practical guides, podcasts, and resources.

## Product purpose

Built with Django is a curated community showcase and learning hub. It should
help visitors discover useful products built with Django, learn from practical
community knowledge, and connect with relevant work, talent, and services.

Success means visitors quickly find real proof that Django is used for serious,
varied products; submitters can keep their own listings accurate; and commercial
surfaces support the site without overwhelming the community content.

## Core journeys

1. Browse, search, filter, and compare published Django projects.
2. Open a project to understand what it does, how it was built, and where to
   learn more.
3. Sign in, submit a project, and update the linked listing and screenshot.
4. Learn through practical guides and conversations with Django builders.
5. Discover Django jobs, developers, makers, and relevant community resources.
6. Use high-intent commercial paths such as job posts, sponsorships,
   advertising, and developer access when relevant.

## Content and ownership model

- Public project discovery is curated. Only published, active, non-spam
  projects belong in public lists and anonymous detail pages.
- A signed-in submitter is linked through `Project.logged_in_maker` and can
  maintain that project's metadata and screenshot.
- Existing `Maker.user` links remain a valid ownership path while the legacy
  maker model is phased down.
- Do not infer ownership from a matching email address or introduce a claiming
  flow without an explicit product decision and abuse-resistant verification.
- Background processing may enrich a submission, but optional enrichment must
  not redefine who owns the listing.

## Brand personality

Friendly, practical, curated, and community-led. The site should feel like a
useful independent Django resource run by a real person, not a generic SaaS
dashboard or corporate developer portal.

## Design principles

1. Lead with useful proof: real projects, screenshots, guides, jobs, and people.
2. Make discovery feel alive: browsing and search should surface relevant,
   current material quickly.
3. Keep the community human: favor clear curation and maker context over
   anonymous content volume.
4. Let submitters maintain accuracy without weakening publication, spam, or
   ownership boundaries.
5. Keep high-intent commercial actions visible but subordinate to the resource
   the visitor came for.
6. Respect mobile browsing and accessibility across project cards, filters,
   navigation, forms, and calls to action.

## Anti-references and non-goals

- Avoid generic AI-generated SaaS design: gradient blobs, interchangeable
  feature grids, decorative dashboard metrics, and vague marketing copy.
- Avoid overplaying Django green or making Django feel childish or unserious.
- Do not hide real project screenshots and community content behind abstract
  decoration.
- Do not turn the public project directory into an unmoderated listing feed.
- Do not add project claiming based only on user-supplied identity data.

## Accessibility and inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, visible keyboard focus, semantic structure, useful alt
text, and touch-friendly controls. Respect reduced motion and keep language
clear for non-native English readers and developers new to Django.
